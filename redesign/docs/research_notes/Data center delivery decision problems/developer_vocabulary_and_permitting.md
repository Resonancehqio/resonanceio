## 3. Lease/SLA language on delivery dates

### Takeaway
Structure is consistent across law-firm sources: target date, outside date, per-hall schedules, escalating credits or liquidated damages, then tenant termination right; force majeure and utility delay carve-outs are the main fight.

### Cited Findings
- V&E, Oct 21, 2026: outside date is "sort of the far outside worst case scenario as to when the project can be completed"; "that's where rent credits or some other liquidated damages start kicking in"; "a day-for-day or multiple-day rent abatement"; landlords often cap credits at "up to maybe one year's worth of rent"; remedy at the end: "the tenant having just a full termination right if the project is not completed by the outside delivery date." — [V&E](https://www.velaw.com/insights/powering-progress-episode-13-power-is-the-product-negotiating-data-center-leases/)
- Troutman Pepper Locke (Carl Bivens), quoted in Law360, Dec 3, 2025: "delay risk is the biggest commercial issue that's negotiated in data center development deals"; hyperscalers "typically want the option to receive liquidated damages based on how much rent they pay" and "a termination right after a certain amount of time passes"; lenders and investors "usually object to a potential lease termination"; developers want carveouts "including utility delays." — [Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/how-energy-scarcity-is-changing-data-center-deals/)
- Holland & Knight, June 2026 (general commercial leases, not data-center-specific): lease "should set an outside date for completion of landlord improvements" and "tie that deadline to a corresponding extension of the rent commencement date"; liquidated damages "commonly accrue daily after the landlord work deadline." — [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/when-landlord-work-is-late-remedies-and-defenses-in-commercial-leases)

### Inferences
- "Target delivery date / outside delivery date" is the native phrase pair; "utility delay" is the contested carve-out.

### Gaps
- Bracewell, DLA Piper, Morgan Lewis not found. No 10-K risk-factor quote on tenant termination for delay fetched. Troutman page was a Law360 summary; the Troutman page itself did not discuss powered shell.

## 4. Permitting and entitlement timelines

### Takeaway
Permitting and approvals alone are commonly put at 6-18 months inside 3-6 year developments, with grid interconnection the longer pole. Source is a single Substack-style trade publication; treat as indicative. No hard survey figure on permitting alone; broader delay-cause data exist.

### Cited Findings
- "permitting and approvals alone often requiring 6-18 months"; full development 3-6 years; "a single resubmission cycle can add three to nine months"; a 12-18 month delay on a $1B project at 7% cost of capital is roughly $70-105M; full EIS "can take two years or more." (Jun 27, 2026) — [Global Data Center Hub](https://www.globaldatacenterhub.com/p/permitting-zoning-and-environmental)
- Same: at least $64B of US projects delayed or blocked by regulatory hurdles and local opposition, May 2024-Mar 2025; Loudoun County VA required a Special Exception in March 2025 (previously by-right since 2000); Virginia DEQ diesel generator air permit applications on/after July 1, 2026 must meet "Tier 4-equivalent controls"; NY legislature passed a one-year moratorium on 20 MW+ data centers on June 4, 2026, pending Governor signature. — same URL
- Sightline Climate via Latitude Media, Feb 24, 2026: "Between 30% and 50% of large data centers scheduled to come online this year" expected delayed, "due to power constraints, equipment shortages, and local opposition"; study tracked 777 projects >50 MW announced since Jan 2024. — [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/up-to-half-of-the-worlds-data-centers-may-be-delayed-this-year/)
- (search-snippet only) Rezoning 1-12 months, site plan 2-6, building permit 1-4, interconnection 6-60 months — [datacentres.com guide](https://www.datacentres.com/guides/data-centre-zoning-permitting); ALL4 air-permit webinar Aug 2021 (about 3 months, 4-6 months, over a year by state) — [ALL4 PDF](https://www.all4inc.com/content/uploads/2021/08/Air-Permitting-and-Planning-Data-Center-Webinar-ALL4-August-2021.pdf)
- (search-snippet only) Uptime Institute Dec 2024: manufacturing and equipment delays the leading factor in capital project delays; 43% reported project delays — [Uptime PDF](https://datacenter.uptimeinstitute.com/rs/711-RIA-145/images/2024.Spending.Survey.3Pager.pdf?version=0)

### Inferences
- The strongest hook for a decision product: delay cost framed as carry cost ($70-105M per 12-18 months on $1B) plus the 3-9 month resubmission cycle.

### Gaps
- No fetched sources on Phoenix, Atlanta, Columbus, Prince William specifics, FAA/noise, or water permit durations (article gave none). No permitting-specific survey percentage found.
