# Power, gear and decisions delay US data centers

**Bottom line.** US data center delivery in 2024–2026 is slowed less by any single failure than by long, serial gates that sit under different owners (utility, OEM, county, EPC, commissioning agent, tenant) and are managed in disconnected tools. The best-sourced evidence: Dominion told developers that connections over 100 MW would take **roughly 4–7 years** instead of 3–4 ([DCK/Bloomberg, Aug 2024](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/data-centers-face-seven-year-wait-for-power-hookups-in-virginia)); Wood Mackenzie puts large power transformers at **128 weeks** and generator step-up units at **144 weeks** (Q2 2025) ([POWER, Jan 2026](https://www.powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis)); and Sightline found **26% of 110 projects** due online in 2025 were delayed, with **30–50%** of 2026 capacity expected to slip ([Latitude Media, Feb 2026](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/up-to-half-of-the-worlds-data-centers-may-be-delayed-this-year/)). Delay is the commercial centre of gravity: a Troutman partner calls it "the biggest commercial issue that's negotiated in data center development deals" ([Troutman, Dec 2025](https://www.troutman.com/insights/how-energy-scarcity-is-changing-data-center-deals/)). On the AI side, every major survey shows a large pilot-to-production gap and thin governance, but the denominators differ and must not be pooled. The weak points in the evidence are equally important for a sales pitch: there is **no public, independent, fully sourced "cost of a late megawatt"**, no source that apportions delay days by cause, no evidence of the systems Related Digital or CyrusOne actually run, and several popular figures (Bank of America "1,900 agents", "$14.2M/month for 60 MW") could not be substantiated. This report uses the researchers' tags: **[F]** means the figure was read on a fetched page; **[S]** means it appeared only in a search snippet and should not be quoted as established.

## Executive summary: the ten most quotable, well-sourced facts

All ten below are tagged [F] in the research notes. Context and caveats follow each.

1. **Northern Virginia power: 4–7 years.** Dominion said connection time for data centers over 100 MW would rise by 1–3 years from a prior 3–4, to roughly four to seven ("as long as seven years") ([DCK/Bloomberg, 30 Aug 2024](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/data-centers-face-seven-year-wait-for-power-hookups-in-virginia)). Dominion's own current 2026 timeline was not found.
2. **Transformers: about 50 weeks (2021) to about 120 (2024) to 128 weeks power / 144 weeks GSU (Q2 2025).** US domestic supply meets only about 20% of demand; prices are up 60–80% since January 2020 ([Wood Mackenzie, Apr 2024](https://www.woodmac.com/es/news/opinion/supply-shortages-and-an-inflexible-market-give-rise-to-high-power-transformer-lead-times/); [POWER, Jan 2026](https://www.powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis)).
3. **Projects slip as a matter of course.** 26% of 110 projects expected online in 2025 were delayed; 30–50% of large data centers scheduled for 2026 are expected to slip, from power constraints, equipment shortages, local opposition, labor and permitting ([Latitude Media, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/up-to-half-of-the-worlds-data-centers-may-be-delayed-this-year/)). Network World attributes identical numbers to "Currence"; the same dataset under two names is unresolved.
4. **The utility's own process runs 3–5 years.** ComEd's published timeline for an on-site customer substation is about **36–60+ months** (cluster study 12–18+ months, detailed engineering 12–18+, construction 12–24+), with construction "contingent on the customer having all required civil construction completed and approved" ([ComEd/JLL deck, Sep 2025](https://mayorscaucus.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Data-Centers-and-ComEd-Power-Overview-ComEd-JLL-9-16-25.pdf)).
5. **Phoenix: the utility stopped promising.** APS has made no commitments to serve additional data center customers since 1 January 2024, with about 4,000 MW already committed ([KJZZ, 2 Sep 2026](https://www.kjzz.org/business/2026-09-02/aps-says-it-cant-commit-to-new-data-centers-because-of-electric-grid-constraints)).
6. **Finished buildings sit dark.** Digital Realty's SJC37 (430,000 sq ft, 48 MW, applied 2019) was vacant awaiting power; Stack's 551,000 sq ft Santa Clara building had empty data halls, with a $450M Silicon Valley Power upgrade due 2028 ([DCK/Bloomberg, 10 Nov 2025](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/data-centers-in-nvidia-s-hometown-stand-empty-awaiting-power)).
7. **Delay is the central negotiated risk.** "Delay risk is the biggest commercial issue that's negotiated in data center development deals"; hyperscalers want liquidated damages tied to rent plus a termination right; developers want utility-delay carve-outs ([Troutman via Law360, 3 Dec 2025](https://www.troutman.com/insights/how-energy-scarcity-is-changing-data-center-deals/)).
8. **MIT NANDA (July 2025): only 5% of custom enterprise AI tools reach production**, with 60% evaluated and 20% reaching pilot; the stated barrier is that systems "do not retain feedback, adapt to context, or improve over time". Sample: 52 interviews, 153 survey responses, 300+ initiatives; the headline "95% failure" is press framing of "no measurable return" ([Virtualization Review, 19 Aug 2025](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2025/08/19/mit-report-finds-most-ai-business-investments-fail-reveals-genai-divide.aspx)).
9. **IDC/Lenovo: 88% of observed POCs do not reach wide-scale deployment; "for every 33 AI POCs launched, only four reached production"** ([CIO.com, 25 Mar 2025](https://www.cio.com/article/3850763/88-of-ai-pilots-fail-to-reach-production-but-thats-not-all-on-it.html)). No sample size stated.
10. **CIOs cannot prove agent outcomes.** In a Dataiku/Harris survey of 685 CIOs, 72% cannot consistently confirm agents deliver intended business outcomes, 81% lack complete oversight of agents built outside formal channels, and 60% lack a central AI governance layer ([The Next Web, Sep 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/cio-ai-agent-oversight-dataiku-survey)). Vendor-sponsored.

## Delivery fails at the seams between power, equipment, permits and contracts

The strongest pattern across the notes is that long-lead items run on separate clocks that nobody owns end to end. Start with power. ComEd's large-load applications grew from 94 MW (2018) to 3,931 MW (2024) to **22,673 MW (2025)**, yet only about 100 projects had paid deposits ([ComEd/JLL deck](https://mayorscaucus.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Data-Centers-and-ComEd-Power-Overview-ComEd-JLL-9-16-25.pdf)). ERCOT is tracking about **474 GW** of large-load requests (June 2026), about 90% data centers; Batch Zero requires $50,000/MW of financial security and evidence such as long-lead purchase orders, land-use approvals and construction agreements ([ERCOT, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/07/29/ERCOT-Senate-July-29-Panel-1-Assessing-The-Grid.pdf)). Oncor's service requests reached 298 GW, about 44 GW qualified for Batch Zero, and the governor paused new interconnection approvals in August 2026 pending an audit, with ERCOT putting the Batch Zero review on hold ([Utility Dive, 7 Aug 2026](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/fate-of-oncors-nearly-300-gw-load-pipeline-unclear-following-texas-data-ce/827303/)). Queue sizes are mostly speculative requests; deliverable megawatts are far smaller. Georgia is the counter-example: Southern Co. has **17 GW contracted**, including a 3.2 GW, 25-year OpenAI contract with service starting 2028 ([Utility Dive, 4 Aug 2026](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/southern-co-contracted-large-load-data-centers/826919/)).

Tariffs have moved risk onto developers. AEP Ohio's PUCO order requires loads over 25 MW to pay at least **85% of subscribed capacity for up to 12 years** ([POWER, Jul 2025](https://www.powermag.com/regulator-approves-aep-ohios-landmark-data-center-tariff/)); Dominion's GS-5 class uses 14-year contracts with 85% minimum demand on distribution and transmission and 60% on generation, effective January 2027 ([American Action Forum, Jan 2026](https://www.americanactionforum.org/insight/virginias-new-data-center-electricity-rate-class/)). These rules change what "contracted capacity" means commercially. A coordination layer needs a state field for every tranche of megawatts, because the same "MW" is used for inquiry, application, studied, contracted and energized capacity (an inference from the sources, not a published taxonomy).

Equipment compounds the power clock. Beyond Wood Mackenzie, trade sources quote switchgear at 40–80+ weeks (unsourced range from [IPS, Jul 2026](https://www.ips.co/news/the-hidden-risk-in-data-center-growth-power-equipment-lead-times)), with a concrete example of 15 kV metal-clad gear at 60 weeks or longer and the observation that much of the quoted lead is slot queue plus submittal and decision delay rather than manufacturing ([Archdesk, vendor blog, 2026](https://archdesk.com/blog/guide-to-long-lead-data-center-equipment)). That last point matters for Resonance: owner and EPC decision latency is a controllable part of lead time. Large diesel and gas generators are quoted at 12–24 months for certain models ([Global Power, vendor blog, Nov 2025](https://www.globalpwr.com/blog/why-planning-ahead-for-backup-power-equipment-matters/)). Vertiv ended 2025 with a **$15.0B backlog (up 109%)** and a Q4 book-to-bill of about 2.9x, though the release gives no lead times ([Vertiv 8-K, Feb 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1674101/000167410126000006/exhibit991vrt02112026.htm)). Treat lead times by equipment class: JLL's relayed averages of 33 weeks globally and 42 weeks in the US (83% above 2019), with chart-read transformers near 43 weeks in the Americas, are a secondary relay and clearly measure a different class than WoodMac's 128 weeks ([NextMSC](https://www.nextmsc.com/industry-deep-dive/tariffs-vs-component-shortages)). No citable lead time exists for UPS, chillers, CDUs or busway.

Permitting and entitlement add procedural risk that is hard to forecast. The Prince William Digital Gateway rezoning (2,100+ acres, 37 data centers, QTS-linked) was approved in December 2023 and voided in August 2025 because the county's advertised public notice did not comply with state or county code ([WTOP, 9 Aug 2025](https://wtop.com/prince-william-county/2025/08/judge-voids-digital-gateway-rezoning-in-prince-william-county/)). A trade newsletter puts permitting and approvals at 6–18 months within 3–6 year developments, with a single resubmission cycle adding three to nine months; this is a single-source, indicative figure ([Global Data Center Hub, 27 Jun 2026](https://www.globaldatacenterhub.com/p/permitting-zoning-and-environmental)). Virginia's presumptive generator NOx limit (0.60 g/hp-hr for applications on or after 1 Jul 2026) is [S] only. No source documents typical permit timelines by jurisdiction.

## Problem map by role group

Each group lists the best-supported facts and the vocabulary that group uses. Vocabulary marked (GP) is general construction-management usage written by the researchers, not drawn from a fetched source; verify with practitioners before publishing.

### 1. Procurement & equipment delivery

Best facts: transformer lead-time trend and supply deficit (30% for power transformers, 10% for distribution in 2025) from Wood Mackenzie via POWER, with new capacity not arriving until Siemens Energy Charlotte (early 2027), Eaton SC (2027) and Hitachi Energy South Boston (2028) ([POWER](https://www.powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis)). Practice is shifting to reserving factory slots with non-refundable deposits before permits are granted; one opinion newsletter says ordering electrical hardware after financial close and the system impact study "no longer works" ([Global Data Center Hub, Sep 2026](https://www.globaldatacenterhub.com/p/order-the-transformer-before-you), opinion, no primary sources). ComEd itself takes advance customer deposits to secure manufacturing slots. For owner-furnished equipment, job postings show the burden: an OFCI Integration Lead owns "the end to end technical and commissioning lifecycle" of owner-furnished, contractor-installed gear, aligning FAT and SAT with commissioning levels ([Bird Construction posting](https://www.careerbeacon.com/en/job-22/3512297/bird-construction/ofci-integration-lead)). Who bears delay on OFCI gear is a seam: the researchers infer the owner carries it, but no fetched source states contract-level allocation. Uptime's 2025 survey says operators reported fewer supply chain disruptions than any of the prior five years but that delays persist (teaser page only, no figures) ([Uptime Data Report 189](https://intelligence.uptimeinstitute.com/resource/supply-chains-show-signs-stability-delays-persist)). This tension should be reflected honestly: the pain is real but not uniformly worsening.

Vocabulary: long-lead item; OFCI; factory slot; split release; hold point; submittal; EoR; VCO (vendor change order); buy-ready sheet; FAT/FWT; SAT; slot reservation; recovery plan; expedite (GP); reallocation (GP); laydown (GP).

### 2. Construction delivery & scheduling

Best facts: the delay-rate band (26% of projects in 2025 per Sightline; 57% with a construction delay of three months or more per JLL 2026 Outlook, secondary relay with cause unidentified; nPlan's vendor claim that 60% slip more than three months) is a band, not one number, because definitions differ (project count vs MW; delayed vs missed; global vs US). Sightline/Currence add that another 10% of expected 2025 capacity quietly postponed commercial operation dates and that, of roughly 16 GW scheduled for 2026, only about 5 GW was under construction ([Network World, 27 Jul 2026](https://www.networkworld.com/article/4201941/up-to-50-of-data-center-capacity-slated-for-2026-could-be-delayed.html)). CBRE counted 5,994.4 MW under construction in primary markets at end-2025, down from 6,350.1 MW ([Power Engineering, Mar 2026](https://www.power-eng.com/business/data-centers-shattered-records-in-2025-so-why-is-construction-slowing-down/)). On how scheduling is run: Primavera P6 is the named schedule tool in owner's-rep postings, with weekly updates involving project managers, field teams, designers and commissioning leads, long-lead procurement tracking, and the duty to "maintain baseline integrity" ([Stelic posting](https://www.builtinboston.com/job/senior-project-scheduler/9705975)). Procore is evidenced at Compass Datacenters and Mortenson ([Compass case study](https://www.casestudies.com/company/procore-technologies/case-study/compass-datacenters-streamlines-10m-sq-ft-of-builds-with-procore)). Turner reports FY2025 revenue of $29.2B, record backlog of $44.3B, about 37% data centers ([Turner, 3 Mar 2026](https://turnerconstruction.com/insights/turner-achieves-record-revenue-and-backlog-in-2025)).

Vocabulary: critical path; baseline / re-baseline; float (GP); look-ahead schedule (GP); pull planning (GP); OAC meeting (GP); recovery plan; delay analysis; hall-by-hall schedule; under construction; pre-leased.

### 3. MEP installation & modular execution

Best facts are thin and mostly vendor-sourced. Prefab/modular claims: Build Technologies says prefab compresses timelines 30–50%, cuts 20–30 MW M&E from about 18 months to 9–11, costs 5–10% more per MW, and can be erased by poor grade, laydown, crane access or transport limits ([Build, May 2026](https://build.inc/insights/modular-data-center-development-prefab), advisory firm with a stake). Datacentres.com Research gives 12–20 weeks for modular components and says "modular does not solve power procurement" ([Datacentres.com, Sep 2026](https://www.datacentres.com/news/factory-built-and-fast-modular-construction-cuts-data-centre-delivery-times-by-4-slot1-2026-09-02)). The numbers across these sites are partly circular; none is independent project-level data. On field quality, Build states "on-site MEP rework is a consistent driver of commissioning delays" (no number). Rework data are general construction, not data center: roughly 2–10% of cost depending on definition, Love (2026) recording under 1% with contractors under-reporting ([PlanRadar compilation](https://planradar.com/us/cost-of-rework-construction/), vendor, secondhand). Do not present a data-center rework rate; none was found.

Vocabulary: prefab / skid / eHouse; modular hall; stick-built; laydown; crane access; transport screen; point-to-point; interface to backbone; MEP rework.

### 4. Cost assurance & capital control

Best facts: owner-side cost control is job-description evidence, not disclosed practice. A CBRE owner's-rep posting for data center construction lists "Create and establish the Master Project Budget," monthly or quarterly cash-flow review, and "Review, negotiate, and recommend on change orders, including 'delay analysis'" using Google Workspace, Office (Excel) and P6 ([The Muse](https://www.themuse.com/jobs/cbre/owners-representative-construction-project-manager)). Stelic adds "audit-ready documentation and narratives for owner oversight." Cost per MW is scope-dependent and should never be mixed across sources: Turner & Townsend 2025–26 Silicon Valley $13.3/W (verified on the T&T page); JLL shell-and-core 2026 ranges of $10–14M/MW by market; Cushman & Wakefield 2026 all-in US/Canada about $17.6M/MW with costs up 21% per MW since Q4 2024 (secondary relays, [NextMSC](https://www.nextmsc.com/industry-deep-dive/data-centre-cost-per-mw-by-region), [IREI](https://irei.com/news/cushman-wakefield-releases-2026-data-center-development-cost-guide-citing-21-rise-in-per-mw-construction-costs/)). ConstructConnect reports year-to-date 2026 starts of $84.1B across 136 projects and average cost of $818.20 per sq ft (+57%) ([ConstructConnect, 28 Aug 2026](https://news.constructconnect.com/september-2026-data-center-report-year-to-date-spending-nearly-three-times-a-year-ago)). Contract tools: liquidated damages run "thousands to tens of thousands of dollars per day" on large projects, supply chain disruption is not automatically force majeure, and contractors seek caps and grace periods ([Bracewell, Dec 2025](https://www.bracewell.com/resources/managing-contractual-risk-in-data-center-construction/)).

Vocabulary: master project budget; soft/hard costs; cash flow forecast; change order; delay analysis; EAC and ETC (GP); contingency drawdown (GP); allowance (GP); liquidated damages; force majeure; price escalation clause; cost per MW ($/W).

### 5. Development feasibility & power

Best facts are in the section above: Dominion 4–7 years; ComEd 36–60+ months and 12–36+ months for distribution-served loads under about 50 MW; APS closed; AEP Ohio's 85%/12-year tariff and a moratorium on new data center service agreements since March 2023; ERCOT Batch process; Georgia contracting at scale. JLL's 2026 outlook as relayed says about 7 years for a 100 MW connection in Northern Virginia and a national average of about 4 years, versus 12–18 months for large commercial loads before 2022 ([Construction Owners Club, Jul 2026](https://www.constructionowners.com/insights/how-long-it-actually-takes-to-power-a-data-center-in-2026-a-u-s-market-by-market-reality-check), secondary). CBRE says power in most markets is largely committed through 2030 and best sites offer power in 18–36 months ([Power Engineering](https://www.power-eng.com/business/data-centers-shattered-records-in-2025-so-why-is-construction-slowing-down/)). Bridge power (behind-the-meter gas, diesel, batteries, fuel cells while awaiting the grid) is defined but has no published durations or costs ([Microgrid Knowledge, Jul 2026](https://www.microgridknowledge.com/distributed-energy/article/55389629/mind-the-gap-and-beyond-why-bridge-power-is-more-than-a-stopgap-measure)). There is no primary wait-time figure in years for SRP, Georgia Power, AEP Ohio or the Pacific Northwest. Related Digital describes itself as "a vertically integrated data center development and investment platform" with a $45B, 5 GW+ near-term pipeline and in-house "entitlement and zoning" and "site selection and land development" ([Related Companies release, 27 Mar 2025](https://www.related.com/press-releases/2025-03-27/related-companies-accelerates-push-data-centers-capitalization-related)).

Vocabulary: time to power; load ramp schedule; request / study / contracted / energized; LLIS; ILLE; T(D)SP; QSA; Load Commissioning Plan; CIAC; cluster study; ESS (ComEd electric service station); minimum demand charge / take-or-pay; exit fee; GS-5; bridge power / BTM; stranded power; power letter of intent; flexible demand response.

### 6. Engineering decisions & permits

Best facts: ComEd's submittal conditions (site control, engineering deposit, service application, load ramp schedule, one-line diagram, site plan; voltage tiers 12 kV to 10 MW, 34 kV 10–50 MW, 138 kV 50–300 MW, 345 kV above 300 MW with customer-owned switchyard) show that engineering choices are utility gates ([ComEd/JLL deck](https://mayorscaucus.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Data-Centers-and-ComEd-Power-Overview-ComEd-JLL-9-16-25.pdf)). ERCOT treats land-use approvals and construction agreements as queue eligibility evidence, so permits are gates for power. The Digital Gateway case shows a procedural defect voiding an approved rezoning; later appeal outcomes in the notes are [S] with conflicting details. It is an inference, not a documented rule, that changing MW, voltage or the one-line diagram can reopen the utility study or an air permit. Commissioning Matters lists "late owner- or engineer-driven sequence changes that contradict already-tested logic" among reasons tests slip ([Commissioning Matters, Jun 2025](https://cxmatters.substack.com/p/racing-the-clock-the-challenges-of)), the clearest link from engineering decisions to downstream cost.

Vocabulary: RFI; submittal; EoR; one-line diagram; IFC (issued for construction); OPR; sequence of operation; synthetic-minor / Title V air permit; special exception; rezoning; by-right; moratorium; resubmission cycle.

### 7. Commissioning & quality

Best facts: level numbering is not standardized (IPS, DAOU and Commissioning Matters describe five-level, five-level with different L2/L3 labels, and seven-level schemes respectively), so never assert one scheme ([IPS](https://www.ipsdb.com/happenings/insights/commissioning-data-centers); [DAOU](https://blog.daouidc.com/en-us/blog/data-centre-commissioning)). The only quantified duration is IST of 4–6 weeks for an air-cooled hall versus 10–14 weeks for a liquid-cooled AI hall, from one undated vendor blog. Slip causes are well described but unmeasured: overlapping design and construction, late chillers, generators and UPS, controls "installed but not integrated with BAS/BMS sequences," unvalidated fire alarm cause-and-effect, and systems "expected to happen in days, not weeks." One staffing-firm author estimates controls issues at 20–40% of commissioning findings ([iRecruit, Aug 2026](https://www.irecruit.co/insights/bas-commissioning-specialist-data-centers)), a single-author estimate. Uptime Institute (Jan 2026) says best practices for commissioning liquid-cooled environments "are still maturing," with CDU makers sometimes new to data centers and factory witness testing "essential" ([Uptime Update 452](https://intelligence.uptimeinstitute.com/pdf-download/node/154022)). No independent data exist on how often L4/L5 tests are rescheduled or fail.

Vocabulary: FAT/FWT; SAT; pre-functional checklist; dead test / live test; startup; FPT; IST; black building test; load bank; BAS/BMS/EPMS/SCADA/DCIM; point-to-point; cause-and-effect; sequence of operation; issues log; go/no-go gate; "go green"; TCS; hydrotest; flushing.

### 8. Customer-ready space & operations

Best facts: turnover is accepted "through evidence, not just physical completion," across six evidence streams, with open punch items blocking turnover and failed IST requiring corrective action and retest evidence ([Build, May 2026](https://build.inc/insights/data-center-closeout-documentation-ai)). The pain is usability: handover packages sit in PDF folders unlinked to equipment tags with conflicting drawing revisions, engineers spending "10–20 minutes locating the correct drawing for each corrective event," and "orphaned commissioning data" ([VueOps, Jan 2026](https://www.vueops.com/knowledge-center/the-hidden-risk-in-data-center-handover), vendor). Uptime's 2025 outage analysis found nearly 40% of organizations had a major human-error outage in three years, with 85% of those stemming from staff not following procedures or flawed procedures ([Uptime press release, May 2025](https://uptimeinstitute.com/about-ui/press-releases/uptime-announces-annual-outage-analysis-report-2025)); this is operations evidence, with no proven causal link to construction handover. Phased energization (halls live one at a time, for example a 100 MW facility starting at 10 MW) carries stranded-capacity and voltage-disturbance risks ([DCK, 28 Jul 2026](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/what-is-phased-energization-and-when-should-data-centers-adopt-it-)). No source defines "ready for customer installation," "hall release," or zone cooling readiness; no frequency data exist for turnover-driven acceptance delay.

Vocabulary: turnover package; closeout register; as-builts; O&M manuals; MOPs; punch list; evidence layer; early-life failures; phased energization; hall release (undefined in sources); white space (undefined in fetched sources).

### 9. Customer commitments & site execution

Best facts: lease structure is consistent across law firms. V&E: "target delivery date" is "the best guess estimate"; each data hall can have its own target and outside delivery date; the outside date is where "rent credits or some other liquidated damages start kicking in," landlord credit caps run "up to maybe one year's worth of rent," and the end remedy is a full tenant termination right; "perhaps power is the product in the data center lease" ([V&E podcast ep. 13, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.velaw.com/insights/powering-progress-episode-13-power-is-the-product-negotiating-data-center-leases/)). Holland & Knight (general commercial leases, not data-center-specific) says to tie the completion deadline to rent commencement ([H&K, Jun 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/when-landlord-work-is-late-remedies-and-defenses-in-commercial-leases)). Investor language pairs three numbers: Digital Realty reported 437 MW "underway," 65% pre-leased, about 10.6% estimated stabilized yield (12.3% on 178 MW in the Americas), and warned of "delays in the estimated start and/or completion date" ([DLR 8-K, 7 May 2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001297996/000119312524133294/d831183d8k.htm)), which is a 2024 figure. Powered shell is defined differently by source: Stream calls it a new building or specialized retrofit built as a data center, typically fed from two substations ([Stream glossary](https://www.streamdatacenters.com/resource-library/glossary/powered-shell/)), while another source calls it a completed building not yet energized. The contradiction is unresolved; ask the customer which they mean.

Vocabulary: target delivery date; outside delivery date; per-hall delivery; rent commencement; rent abatement / credits; liquidated damages; termination right; utility-delay carve-out; force majeure; pre-leased; projects underway; estimated stabilized yield; development capacity; powered shell; critical IT load (S only); RFS (not defined in any fetched source).

## The cost of a late megawatt has no clean public number

No fetched source gives an authoritative per-megawatt delay cost, and the figures in circulation rest on different bases, so the pitch should present a transparent method rather than a headline. What is sourced: nPlan, a vendor, states "the maths on a 100MW build: $27–65m of deferred revenue before liquidated damages or a broken SLA" for a three-month slip, with method not shown, and separately says "a $1 billion facility routinely becomes a $1.3 billion one" ([nPlan briefing](https://www.nplan.io/white-papers/nplan-executive-briefing-the-data-center-delivery-problem), vendor marketing). Global Data Center Hub computes that a 12–18 month delay on a $1B project at 7% cost of capital is roughly **$70–105M** (simple carry arithmetic, indicative, newsletter) ([GDCH, Jun 2026](https://www.globaldatacenterhub.com/p/permitting-zoning-and-environmental)). CBRE's H2 2025 record asking rate of **$196.25/kW-month** for 250–500 kW requirements (3–10 MW up 12.5% YoY; Hillsboro 3–10 MW $167.50) is [S] only because the CBRE page returned 403 ([CBRE](https://www.cbre.co.uk/insights/books/north-america-data-center-trends-h2-2025)). Contractual exposure: LDs of thousands to tens of thousands of dollars per day ([Bracewell](https://www.bracewell.com/resources/managing-contractual-risk-in-data-center-construction/)); tenant rent credits capped around a year of rent plus termination rights (V&E, Troutman). Capex context: $9.5–17.6M per MW depending on scope and source.

The researchers' own arithmetic (not sourced): $196.25/kW-month times 1,000 is about $196k per MW-month asking rent, so 100 MW slipping three months is about $59M at small-requirement asking rates, inside nPlan's $27–65M band. Because hyperscale contract rents sit below small-requirement asking rates, a band of roughly $0.1–0.2M per MW-month of deferred revenue is defensible but should be labelled as derived. By the same arithmetic, the unverified "$14.2M per month for 60 MW" implies about $237k per MW-month, above the CBRE asking rate; that is a further reason not to use it. It appears in a DAOU vendor blog and a Reality Capture Experts snippet with no named source. The recommended pitch device is a calculator that takes the buyer's own rent, ramp and carry, not a borrowed number. Missing: any per-day LD figure from a filing, hyperscale contract rent per kW, financing carry per MW, and developer or REIT delay-penalty disclosures.

## The AI-to-production gap is a governance gap, and the surveys measure different things

The surveys agree on direction and disagree on denominators. MIT NANDA measures measurable P&L return (95% see none; 5% of custom enterprise tools reach production) ([Virtualization Review](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2025/08/19/mit-report-finds-most-ai-business-investments-fail-reveals-genai-divide.aspx)). IDC/Lenovo measures POC-to-scale (88% do not; four of 33 reach production) ([CIO.com](https://www.cio.com/article/3850763/88-of-ai-pilots-fail-to-reach-production-but-thats-not-all-on-it.html)). Deloitte's 3,235-leader survey finds about a quarter say at least 40% of AI experiments have reached production, and projects scoped at three months can take 18+ months, the "proof-of-concept trap" ([Unite.AI, 21 Jan 2026](https://www.unite.ai/state-of-ai-in-the-enterprise-2026-deloitte-maps-the-untapped-edge-of-enterprise-ai/), secondary relay). Gartner forecasts that more than 40% of agentic AI projects could be canceled by end-2027 on cost, unclear value and weak risk controls, from a poll of 3,412 webinar attendees, and says only about 130 agentic vendors are genuine ("agent washing") ([w.media, Jun 2025](https://w.media/over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-could-face-the-axe-by-end-of-2027-gartner/)). Pick one statistic, quote it with its scope, and never blend them. McKinsey (23% scaling an agentic system, 88% using AI in at least one function), S&P Global (42% abandoned most initiatives) and Gartner's governance survey (13% "strongly agreed" they have the right governance structures; 19% high trust in vendor hallucination protection) are all [S] only and should not be quoted as established.

Causes are non-model: IDC cites low organizational readiness in data, processes and infrastructure, unclear ROI and underfunded POCs; MIT cites systems that do not retain feedback or adapt to context. Governance evidence from the fetched Dataiku/Harris survey of 685 CIOs: 72% cannot consistently confirm agents deliver intended outcomes even though 90% say they track all agents, 83% lack standardized lifecycle management, 84% say employees create agents faster than IT can govern, and only 21% have near-real-time cost visibility ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/cio-ai-agent-oversight-dataiku-survey), vendor-sponsored). Deloitte: only 21% have a mature governance model for autonomous agents; top risks are privacy/security 73%, legal/regulatory 50%, oversight 46%. The EU AI Act's Article 12 (automatic event logging) and Article 14 (human overseers who can intervene, override or decline an output) map onto Resonance's audit record and accountable owner, but those readings are [S] and the 2 August 2026 high-risk date was not checked for delay proposals; use them as framing, not as a US compliance claim. NIST AI RMF was not researched. Evidence is thin on the exact Resonance requirements: no survey tests demand for deterministic solvers, and nothing quantifies decision governance on $500M-scale capital projects.

For banks, the only sourced figure is that Bank of America has deployed an Agentforce-based internal platform to "around 1,000 financial advisers" with 18,000 developers using AI coding tools ([NASCUS, 26 Mar 2026](https://www.nascus.org/2026/03/26/ai-agents-enter-banking-roles-at-bank-of-america/), secondary summary). The "1,900 agents, one in production" claim has no source and must not be used.

The "$14.2M/month for 60 MW" commissioning-delay figure: it appears only in a DAOU vendor blog and a Reality Capture Experts snippet, with no named source, and implies a per-MW-month rate above CBRE's small-requirement asking rent. ## Conclusion

The research changes the pitch in two ways. First, the credible problem is not "supply chain" in the abstract but the coupling of clocks: utility studies that wait on the customer's civil completion (ComEd), permits that become queue-eligibility evidence (ERCOT), tariffs that convert delay into minimum-take exposure (AEP Ohio, Dominion), and leases that convert delay into rent credits and termination (V&E, Troutman). A decision layer earns its place at those handoffs, where the information is split across owners and tools, and Archdesk's observation that quoted lead time is largely queue plus decision latency is the strongest evidence that part of the delay is controllable.

Second, the honest evidence base is narrower than the industry's loudest statistics. There is no clean cost-of-a-late-MW figure, no data on delay by cause, and no proof of what systems the two target buyers use. The more persuasive web copy and sales pitch will therefore use the buyer's own vocabulary (outside delivery date, hold point, load ramp schedule, IST, turnover package), cite only [F] figures with their scope and date, show a calculator driven by the buyer's rent and ramp rather than a borrowed number, and put the interview questions above to CyrusOne and Related Digital before asserting anything about their internal tools or pain.
