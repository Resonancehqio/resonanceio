# Critique of the Resonance homepage plan, from the seat of an EPC CEO

Written 26 Oct 2026. Role: CEO of a large US EPC whose backlog is mostly data centers. Reads: the homepage brief, MOCKUP-PLAN.md, PRODUCT.md, the delivery-problems report, the founder transcript. Primary sources are linked inline. Anything I could not verify is marked **[unverified]**.

## The short version

The plan is built to win an argument about AI. I do not have that argument. I have a different one: which of my late days are mine, which are the owner's, what I can bill for, and where my crews go next. The homepage should show Resonance understands that problem first, show the mechanism second, and leave the six disciplines, five modules and three views for the page one click down. The founder's steer is right, and the plan fails it in sections 3, 5, 6 and 8.

## What an EPC CEO actually worries about (primary sources read)

- **Cost overruns on fixed-price work, and schedule guarantees.** Comfort Systems' FY2025 10-K: "we may experience reduced profits or, in some cases, losses under these contracts if costs increase above our estimates"; "Failure to meet schedule or performance requirements typically results in additional costs to us"; "we may also create liability for consequential and liquidated damages" ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1035983/000110465926017530/fix-20251231x10k.htm)). The same filing lists "claims against the customer for increased costs incurred by us due to customer-induced delays" as a recovery it depends on.
- **Recovering money for delay I did not cause.** Sterling's 10-K names "A failure to promptly document and negotiate a recovery for change orders and claims" as a risk factor, and cites amounts sought for "customer-caused delays, errors in specifications and designs" ([Sterling FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/874238/000087423826000024/strl-20251231.htm)). EMCOR: "we fail to successfully negotiate a significant portion of our change orders and/or claims with clients"; it also lists "payment of penalties or liquidated or other damages" and receivables that clients delay paying ([EMCOR FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/105634/000010563426000025/eme-20251231.htm)). Claims and cash are the CEO's and CFO's language, and the plan has neither.
- **Labor is the binding constraint, not software.** Sterling's CEO said the electrician shortage "certainly has curtailed us from taking on more jobs than we could" ([Q2 2026 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/10/sterling-infrastructure-strl-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Comfort's FY2025 10-K lists "Labor shortages, including the recent U.S. labor shortage, increased labor costs" as a risk. A secondary summary of its Q4 2025 call says equipment costs are largely quoted and labor is the main cost risk **[secondary, unverified wording]** ([Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/20/comfort-systems-fix-earnings-call-transcript/)). Quanta flagged tight craft labor in data centers on its Q4 2025 call **[search summary only, not read in full]**.
- **Contract form and margin.** Sterling: "The majority of our revenues and backlog are derived from fixed-unit price contracts and from lump sum contracts." Fluor says 81% of year-end 2025 backlog is reimbursable **[from a search result, 10-K not opened]** ([Fluor FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1124198/000112419826000007/flr-20251231.htm)). EMCOR's mechanical margin fell 110 bps to 12.5% in Q2 2026, tied to more construction-manager/prime work and more GMP and cost-plus **[secondary summary, not read]** ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/emcor-group-q2-earnings-call-highlights)). Comfort's Q1 2026 gross margin of 26.3% included a stated $43M of favourable late closeouts and change orders **[secondary]** ([Nasdaq summary](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/comfort-systems-usa-q2-earnings-call-highlights)); the 8-K itself gives 26.3% but not the $43M ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001035983/000110465926047679/fix-20260423xex99d1.htm)). Read together: margin on these jobs is made or lost in closeout, change orders and claims.
- **Backlog and customer concentration.** Comfort: "Our backlog is subject to unexpected adjustments and cancellations"; one customer was about 12.8% of 2025 revenue. Sterling: "one or a few customers... may in the future represent a substantial portion."
- **Delay allocation in the contract.** Law-firm reading: Stoel Rives lists "Responsibility for procurement versus installation" and "Risk of delay due to manufacturer lead times" as recurring disputes, plus concurrent-delay analysis and constructive-acceleration claims ([Stoel](https://www.stoel.com/insights/publications/does-your-cloud-have-a-silver-lining)). WSHB advises separating "work the contractor controls" from owner-furnished equipment, utility service and access, and warns causation is hard when late switchgear and a late utility connection both hit the critical path ([WSHB](https://www.wshblaw.com/experience-built-but-not-ready-construction-claims-in-the-data-center-boom)). Hamilton Locke: with owner-supplied equipment "responsibility is split across manufacturers, systems integrators and the [EPC]"; keep "contemporaneous delay analyses" ([Hamilton Locke](https://hamiltonlocke.com.au/when-construction-meets-capacity-litigation-risks-in-data-centre-builds/)). Wiss: too-high daily LD rates push subs and suppliers to inflate bids ([Wiss](https://wiss.com/data-center-construction-contracts/)).
- **Forensic standard.** AACE RP 29R-03 is the reference for CPM-based forensic delay analysis, with method protocols 3.1 to 3.9, and says it is "not intended to establish a standard of practice" ([AACE](https://www.pathlms.com/aace/courses/2928/documents/3813)). I read only the summary page, not the RP.

Not found: any 10-K or call from these contractors saying software or a language model is a worry. That is the first signal about the plan's headline.

## 1. The 30-second test

**Book:** a headline in my units that names a decision I am late on, a picture of one late package turning into dollars and days with a "whose delay is it" line, and a CTA that asks for one package, not a platform evaluation.

**Close the tab:** a hero about "decisions your operation can actually execute" (every vendor says this), an animated ERP-workbook loop (a distribution story, not mine), and a second screen that opens with a claim about language models.

**Is "the language model is the smallest contributor" the winning argument?** No, for me. It answers "my people already get this from a chatbot." That is the objection of a CIO, an investor, or a founder's friend. I will not be in the room with that sentence. It also invites my CIO to ask what the model does, which is a question I would rather he ask on the second call. "$1.17" reads as clever to a technologist and as cheap to an executive; it tells me nothing about my money. Move the argument to the platform and security pages, and write one line of it on the home page as reassurance (the model never decides; a named person signs).

The three arguments that win my leadership team:

- **CEO:** "I find out a package is late from a vendor email. I want to see, the week it slips, what it does to the handover date, who owns that day under the contract, and the cheapest option that works." Margin protection and not being the one holding someone else's delay.
- **COO:** days, crews, float. "Which crew goes where when switchgear slips, and does that move the problem to another job?"
- **CFO:** "Show me the exposure in dollars before the claim letter, and the notice and record that let me recover it." LDs, standby cost, unapproved change orders, cash tied up waiting on approval.

Evidence to use, all already in the report: delay is "the biggest commercial issue that's negotiated in data center development deals" ([Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/how-energy-scarcity-is-changing-data-center-deals/)); 26% of 110 projects due online in 2025 were delayed ([Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/up-to-half-of-the-worlds-data-centers-may-be-delayed-this-year/)); LDs of "thousands to tens of thousands of dollars per day" ([Bracewell](https://www.bracewell.com/resources/managing-contractual-risk-in-data-center-construction/)); transformers at 128 weeks ([POWER](https://www.powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis)). Use one, with its scope.

## 2. Four equal doors versus the vertical buyer

Four equal doors dilute. Three reasons. First, a data center EPC CEO sees "Housing & Relocation" and "Supply Chain & Distribution" at equal weight and concludes this is a horizontal tool still looking for its market. Second, only the data center app has real screens. PRODUCT.md says its site is one vertical, one buyer, one scenario; MOCKUP-PLAN.md says mock the other three in the same UI. A buyer who asks to see the distribution app on the call finds a mock-up, and I will remember that. Third, the plan's own principle 2 in PRODUCT.md ("the horizontal platform story lives elsewhere") contradicts the brief's Pallet-model homepage.

**Recommendation:** keep the platform nav and the Pallet model, but make the home page an industry-first page with data centers and EPC as the lead (hero, problem, proof). Add a quiet "Also on the platform" row of the other three at the foot, one line each, linking out. If the other three must sit higher, use a picker in the hero ("I work in: Data centers & EPC · Distribution · Public works · Housing") that rewrites the hero question; default to data centers. The platform story (one engine, many apps) earns one sentence in the home page's last third: "The same engine runs decision apps in other industries." The proof of the platform is that the data center app is deep, not that there are four doors.

## 3. What the plan gets wrong or misses about EPC and data center work

**The scenario is told from the owner's seat.** Alder Creek is a developer's problem: a customer commitment in MW IT, a "Procurement Director" who approves reallocating a package from an uncommitted later phase for $350k. An EPC is usually on the other side of the OFCI line. If the switchgear is owner-furnished, the first questions are: was it OFCI or CFCI, what is my notice date, is this a compensable and excusable delay, and what standby cost and resequencing do I claim? If it is mine, the question is my LD exposure and whether I can recover from the supplier. The plan has no concept of entitlement, notice, concurrent delay or contemporaneous record, yet the legal sources above treat these as the core of delay disputes. "Evidence-linked decision record" is a strong fit; it is currently pitched as audit, not as claim defence. That is the biggest missed positioning.

**Vocabulary gaps.** Present: long-lead, OFCI, float, EAC, IST, MW IT. Missing: LDs and their cap, notice and time-extension, compensable versus excusable delay, concurrent delay, unapproved change orders, standby and demobilization cost, backcharge, retainage, billing milestones and cash, GMP versus lump sum versus cost-plus, margin fade, crew availability by trade. "MW IT-days" is the owner's exposure unit; the EPC's is LD dollars per day and crew-weeks.

**Who buys.** At an EPC the sponsor is the CEO or COO; the evaluator is the head of preconstruction/operations or project controls (the P6 owner); the CFO tests the money; the CIO and legal gate security and data. The plan lists 18 roles, which is a user list, not a buying committee. A CEO wants three: who signs, who uses it, who can say no. The brief also frames the target as an "EPC/operator"; if the second prospect is an owner-operator, the pitch differs and the site must not blur them **[my industry knowledge, not verified against a source]**.

**Does reallocating from a later phase just move the slip?** That is my first question. The sample shows "$350k, 0 days" with "the donor phase's schedule protected." I would want on screen: the donor phase's new slot date, its float, the cost to re-buy, which crew and which commitment it now threatens, and whether the donor's customer has agreed. If the answer is "the donor is uncommitted and has float", say that in one line. If the product cannot show the second-order effect, the option is a deferral, not a recovery. Also: $900k to save 21 days and $350k to save 42 is a suspiciously good trade; I would ask what the $350k excludes (the donor's re-buy, premium freight, requalification). The plan prints "financial benefits are never inferred," which I like; put it beside the number.

**Where an EPC differs from an owner as a buyer.** I buy to protect margin and capacity, not to protect a lease. I sell time to the owner and carry LD risk down to subs. My upside is closeout and change orders; my worst day is a claim I cannot document. My data lives in my project controls and cost systems and in the owner's portal; I will not hand over the owner's confidential schedule. My payback case is margin points on a job and labor redeployed, not MW-days.

## 4. Proof without customer names

What I would need to believe: that this works on my kind of mess, that it never invents a number, and that someone other than the founders can run it. Without names, give me:

- **A method I can audit.** One walkthrough with every figure carrying a record ID, assumptions printed, and a visible "what this does not know" panel. The honesty rails in the plan are the best thing in it; move them up.
- **A downloadable sample pack** (the sample workbook in, the decision record out) so my project controls lead can try it unaided in an afternoon.
- **A calculator that takes my numbers** (my LD rate, my standby cost, my daily crew cost), as the research report already recommends, instead of a borrowed cost-of-delay figure. No public per-day LD benchmark exists in the sources.
- **Written scope of what is claimed and not.** Not a promise on legal entitlement; a record that supports it.

**A working session I would say yes to:** 90 minutes, no data leaving my building. I bring one real late package (a redacted P6 extract and the PO and delivery log, or just the slip and the contract clauses). You return within 5 business days a sample decision record on it: the schedule effect, the options that can execute, the dollars, and a list of what you assumed. Cost and the pilot fee stated before the session, not after. I keep the output either way. If I cannot get that for free, the page should say what the paid pilot covers: one project, a fixed time, named deliverables, and who from my side is needed (the brief's pricing-as-structure approach fits if "3 months" and "one site" are stated). I do not know whether Resonance can deliver the 5-day turnaround; the founders must say. **[Founder confirmation needed.]**

## 5. First-call questions

1. Where does my data go, who can read it, is it used to train anything, and which model vendor sees what? (Founder-meeting notes show the model is meant to see only the mapped data; I want that in writing.)
2. How does it read P6 (XER, API, or export?), SAP or Oracle cost data, Procore and Excel? What does it write back, and can I turn writes off?
3. Who on my team does the work, how many hours a week, and which skills? Who on yours?
4. If the plan is wrong and I act on it, who is liable? What is in your contract on that, and what does your insurance say?
5. How long from signature to the first decision record on my project, and what has to be true on my side?
6. Does it handle my contract types (GMP, lump sum, cost-plus) and OFCI/CFCI split? Where are LDs, caps and notice dates entered, and who owns them?
7. How does it treat concurrent delay and the owner's delay? Is the record built contemporaneously? Does it follow a recognised method such as AACE RP 29R-03, or is it not a forensic tool at all?
8. What happens when the schedule is garbage? What does it say, and does it still produce a plan?
9. Who can approve in the tool, and can my owner's rep see it?
10. What does it cost beyond the pilot, and what do I lose if I stop?

## 6. The rewrite

Principle (founder steer): **problem first, solution second, detail one click deeper.** Seven sections, down from eleven. No feature tour on the home page.

**Section order**

1. **Hero** (problem, my units)
2. **The problem, in three beats** (what actually happens when a long-lead package slips)
3. **What Resonance does about it** (three steps, one picture)
4. **One late package, end to end** (Alder Creek, sample data, whose delay, what it costs, who signs)
5. **What you are not asked to believe** (systems stay yours, a named person signs, nothing inferred, model never decides)
6. **Other industries** (one quiet row; platform one click away)
7. **Bring us one late package** (CTA, then footer)

Dropped from the home page (moved one click down): six disciplines, five modules, the comparison table, pricing structure, the 18 roles, the animated ERP loop, MIT NANDA/IDC stats (the CIO page).

**Copy**

- **Hero:** "A switchgear slip is a handover slip, and someone pays for every day. See who, and what to do about it, the week it happens."
- **Sub-line:** "Resonance reads your schedule, procurement and cost data where it already lives, shows what a late package does to the handover date and to your exposure, and lists only the recovery options that can actually be carried out. A named person approves."
- **CTA primary:** "Bring us one late package." Secondary: "See one end to end."
- **Problem, beat 1:** "The slip arrives as an email. The effect on the critical path arrives weeks later."
- **Beat 2:** "Whose delay it is decides who pays. The owner's equipment, the utility, the supplier, or you: the contract says one thing and the schedule says another, and the record is rebuilt after the fact."
- **Beat 3:** "The fix is chosen in a meeting, from a spreadsheet, with no record of what else it moved." (Cite one sourced line: delay risk is "the biggest commercial issue" in these deals, Troutman.)
- **Solution, three steps:** "It connects the slip to the handover date and the float on the path. It computes only options that can execute: equipment that fits, slots that exist, crews that are free. It records who approved what, on which evidence, so the notice and the decision stand up later."
- **Proof section heading:** "A 24 MW hall, a six-week slip, three ways out." Body: Keep ($0, 42 days late), Expedite ($900k, 21 days late), Reallocate from a later phase ($350k, on time, with what it does to the donor phase shown). Label: "Sample data. Not a customer." Add the second-order line the CEO will ask for: "The donor phase's new date, its float and its re-buy cost are shown beside the saving."
- **Reassurance section:** "Your systems stay yours. A language model helps read your files and answer questions; it never makes the call. Every figure carries a record ID. Benefits are never inferred."
- **Industries:** "The same engine runs decision apps in distribution, public works and housing."
- **CTA section:** "Bring us one late package. Ninety minutes, your data stays with you, and you leave with a sample decision record."

Banned phrases avoided; no customer or team names, no prices, no unsourced statistics; "decides" is used only in the negative.

**Vertical page (one click down)** carries what the home page drops: the whole walkthrough, the roles, the systems, the six disciplines compressed to a short list, and the comparison with a spreadsheet, a dashboard and a chatbot. Add to it a section on contract position (OFCI versus CFCI, notice, concurrent delay) once the product can honestly do it.
