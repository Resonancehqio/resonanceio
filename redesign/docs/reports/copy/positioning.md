# Resonance for EPC contractors: positioning and plain-word copy

Written 26 Oct 2026. Sources read: PRODUCT.md, founder meeting transcript, critique-epc-ceo.md, mockups/CONTENT.md. Fictional sample world only. No prices, customers or real places.

## 0. How this was built

Raw material: the CEO critique (what an EPC CEO loses sleep over: LDs, notice, standby, crews, owner-furnished equipment, claims) and the founders' own plain description ("everything is decisions", "connect data behind every decision", "the humans had to sit and coordinate").

Expert moves applied:
1. **Dunford order.** Alternatives first, then what only we do, then the value, then who cares most, then the shelf. Copy comes last.
2. **Schwartz stage.** The EPC CEO knows the problem (late gear) and knows no product for it. So the headline names the problem in his words and promises the result. No category education up front.
3. **Ogilvy plainness.** Every line must be sayable by a superintendent. Concrete nouns: delivery, handover date, crew, notice, approval.
4. **Buyer's units.** Days late, dollars, crew standby. MW IT-days is the owner's unit. EPC copy leads with days and dollars.
5. **Claims caution.** We say "record". We never say we establish who is at fault. The critique flags this as the biggest hole in the old copy.

The check: the five-second say-back test in section 3. A line survives only if a skeptical COO can say back what it is, correctly, with no help.

## 1. Positioning canvas

**What it is, in one plain sentence.** When something changes on a job (a delivery slips, a design revision lands, a utility date moves), Resonance shows what that does to the handover date and the money, works out which recovery options can actually be built, ties each number to its source document, sends the decision to the person who owns it, and keeps the record.

**Competitive alternatives (what they do today)**

| Today | What is wrong with it |
|---|---|
| Email, then the weekly coordination meeting | The effect on handover shows up weeks after the email. The fix is argued from memory. |
| Scheduler runs a P6 what-if | Takes days. Shows schedule only, not cost, crews or whether the gear fits. |
| Excel recovery workbook built per event | Rebuilt by hand each time. Numbers have no trail back to the document. |
| Ask a chatbot | Fast, but it can suggest a fix that cannot be built, and no number is tied to a source. |
| Do nothing until the owner's letter arrives | The claim record gets rebuilt after the fact. |

**Unique attributes (things only we have)**
- Starts from the change, then follows it to handover date and cost.
- Options are checked by calculation (fit, slots, crews, schedule logic) before anyone sees them. Unbuildable options never appear.
- Every figure carries the ID of the document it came from.
- One named owner gets each decision. Approve, hold or reject, with a reason.
- A dated record of what was known, what was chosen and why.
- Sits on top of P6, SAP, Oracle, Procore and Excel. Nothing is replaced.

**Value (for the EPC)**
- You see the days and dollars at stake when the slip lands, not weeks later.
- You pick from options that can be built, with cost beside each.
- When the owner or a sub disputes the delay, the record is already there.
- Crew moves are priced before they are made.

**Best-fit customer.** A data center EPC with several halls under construction, long-lead gear (owner-furnished or its own), LDs or schedule guarantees in the contract, and the schedule in P6. Sponsor: CEO or COO. Evaluator: head of project controls or preconstruction. Money test: CFO.

**Market category (the shelf).** **Delay response software.** Plain meaning: what you use when a delivery or date slips. It sits next to P6, not on top of the scheduler's job, and is not delay analysis for lawyers.

Shelves rejected: "decision layer / decision OS" (means nothing to a contractor), "project controls" (P6's shelf, reads as a replacement), "delay analysis" (forensic and after the fact), "risk management" (a register, not an answer), anything with "agent".

## 3. Five-second test

Method: read the line cold. A skeptical EPC COO says back "so it's a ___". The line passes only if his say-back is correct, specific and something he would want. Top 10 chosen from sections 2a and 2b.

| # | Line | COO says back | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2a-1 Delay response software for data center contractors. | "So it's software for when stuff slips on my jobs." | **Pass.** Correct and specific. Weak on what it does, fine for a category line. |
| 2 | 2b-1 See what a late delivery costs you, same day. | "So it prices a slip right away." | **Pass.** Right. Needs the subhead for the options and the record. |
| 3 | 2b-2 Supplier slipped? Know the damage by lunch. | "So it tells me the damage fast." | **Pass with edit.** "By lunch" is a speed promise we have not shown. Keep as alternate. |
| 4 | 2b-9 Keep the record your delay claim needs. | "So it's claims paperwork software." | **Narrow pass.** Right but too small: it hides the options and cost. Use as a second-screen line. Also invites "does it replace my claims consultant?" |
| 5 | 2b-8 Recovery options that can actually be built. | "So it's a scheduling tool that suggests fixes." | **Pass for the second screen.** Does not say it starts from the slip. |
| 6 | 2a-8 Prices every slip and keeps the record. | "So it's a cost tracker." | **Kill as lead.** Nothing about options or handover. Fine as a footer line. |
| 7 | 2b-12 Every delay decision has a name on it. | "So it's an approvals tool." | **Kill as lead.** Right but empty of the problem. Good as a trust line. |
| 8 | 2b-13 Know what a slipped package does to your crews. | "So it's crew scheduling." | **Kill as lead.** Wrong shelf. Crew is one decision, not the product. Keep as a use-case card. |
| 9 | 2a-22 Software that tells you which delays you pay for. | "So it decides fault. My lawyers would hate that." | **Kill.** Overpromises. Claims entitlement. |
| 10 | 2a-29 Price the slip, pick the fix, keep the record. | "So it's... a checklist?" | **Kill.** Reads as slogan. Stacked list. Not a thing you can buy. |
| 11 | 2b-21 Fewer days late. Less spent catching up. | "So it's a promise of results. Prove it." | **Kill.** No evidence on hand. Outcome claim with no pilot data. |
| 12 | 2b-24 The cheapest fix that works, with proof. | "So it picks the fix for me?" | **Kill.** Implies the tool chooses. A named person chooses. |

Survivors for the page: 2a-1 (category), 2b-1 (hero), 2b-2 (alternate hero), 2b-15 (calm backup), 2b-9 and 2b-8 (second-screen lines), 2b-12 (trust line).

## 4. Recommendation

**Category line (7 words)**
> Delay response software for data center contractors.

**Hero headline (9 words)**
> See what a late delivery costs you, same day.

**Subhead (22 words)**
> Resonance shows what the slip does to handover and cost, works out only the fixes you can build, and records who approved.

**Elevator line, for a CEO to repeat to the CFO (1 sentence)**
> When a delivery slips on one of our data center jobs, Resonance shows the days and dollars at stake, works out the recovery options we can actually build, and keeps the record of who approved what and why.

**25-word version**
> When a delivery slips, Resonance shows the effect on handover and cost, lists the fixes you can build, and keeps the record.

**50-word version**
> When a delivery slips or a design changes, Resonance shows the effect on handover and cost. It works out the recovery options that can be built, ties each figure to its source document, sends the decision to its owner, and keeps the record. P6, SAP, Oracle, Procore and Excel stay.

**Why this one wins.** The hero names the problem in a contractor's words (late delivery), promises one result (cost, fast), and survives the say-back. The subhead holds the three differences from a chatbot or a P6 what-if: it starts from the slip, checks options can be built, and keeps the record. The words "decide" and "approve" always belong to a person.

## 5. Copy rules and open items

- Lead with days and dollars. Use MW IT-days only for owner-side pages.
- Say "record", never "entitlement" or "who is at fault". The critique's own caution: a record supports a claim, it does not win one.
- Do not claim a speed ("by lunch", "minutes") until a pilot shows it. "Same day" needs founder confirmation too.
- Do not claim saved days or dollars. No pilot data exists. Sample-scenario numbers (fictional, labelled) are fine in the walkthrough.
- One sourced fact is safe to place near the hero if wanted: "26% of 110 data center projects expected online in 2025 were delayed" (Latitude Media, Feb 2026, https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/up-to-half-of-the-worlds-data-centers-may-be-delayed-this-year/). Use only with that scope.
- The sample world for any proof section is the Tamberlane, Norvale, Heron one in mockups/CONTENT.md: owner-furnished switchgear SG-B02 slips 35 days, handover moves 14 May to 18 Jun 2027, crew standby $18,400 a day (fictional, illustrative).
- Open question for the founders: how fast can a first decision record come back on a real late package? The subhead and headline depend on the answer.
