### 1. "My schedulers already do this."

**Website copy (28 words):**
Your schedulers still own the schedule. Resonance does the repeat work when a package slips: the handover effect and buildable options, each figure tied to a record ID.

**Proof that exists:** The Heron walkthrough shows one slip (SG-B02, +35 days, zero float) turned into a new handover date, four options and five record IDs on one screen. The working session lets the scheduler check every line against their own method.

**Note:** Never say it replaces the scheduler. The scheduler is the reader who can say no, and the page must survive their read.

### 2. "We're not ripping out P6."

**Website copy (26 words):**
Nothing is replaced. Resonance sits on top of P6, SAP, Oracle, Procore and Excel and reads what they already hold. Your records stay where they are.

**Proof that exists:** The product positioning (sits on top; customers keep their systems). The sample runs from a sample workbook and a P6-style dependency schedule (SCH-089).

**FOUNDER TO CONFIRM:** how each system is read (export, XER, API) and whether anything is ever written back. The copy says "reads" only, so it stays true either way. Do not add "writes back nothing" until confirmed.

### 3. "We already use ChatGPT."

**Website copy (24 words):**
Keep using it. A language model drafts text here. Solvers do the math, every figure has a record ID, and a named owner approves.

**Proof that exists:** Record IDs on every figure in the walkthrough; the printed assumptions line ("zero float on the affected electrical path; installation and commissioning durations held; costs exclude unmodeled financing, revenue, damages and tax"); the option the solver marks Ineligible with its reason (the air-freighted substitute lineup is not qualified against the Hall B protection design). A chat answer cannot show why an option was ruled out.

**Held back until confirmed:** the model-cost line. "Founder-reported, sample plan: the model cost $1.17, the rest of the compute about $300" is not yet confirmed by a cost log (CONTENT.md says CONFIRM; the CEO critique could not find it in the transcript). Use only with that label, only after the log is seen, and only on the platform or CIO page.

### 4. "Another pilot that goes nowhere."

**Website copy (22 words):**
Start with one late package, in one working session. You leave with a sample decision record on it. No platform rollout comes first.

**Proof that exists:** The working-session offer (90 minutes, your data stays with you, you leave with a sample decision record). DR-3105 shows what the output looks like. The published pilot structure (3 months, one site, two to three apps) can sit on the deeper page with no price.

**FOUNDER TO CONFIRM:** a turnaround after the session (the CEO critique asks for "within 5 business days"). Not in the copy until a founder agrees to it.

### 5. "I won't hand the owner's schedule to a startup."

**Website copy (28 words):**
Your data stays with you in the working session. Bring a redacted extract or just the slip and its dates. Connection to live systems comes later, in writing.

**Proof that exists:** The session terms (data stays with you). The sample is entirely fictional, so nothing of anyone's was used to build it.

**FOUNDER TO CONFIRM:** where data is stored once connected, which model vendor sees what, and whether it trains anything. These are the CEO's first-call question 1 and need a written answer before the security page goes live. Until then, "in writing" is a commitment to produce that answer, so confirm it is one you will keep.

### 6. "Who's liable if it's wrong?"

**Website copy (28 words):**
A named owner signs every decision. Each figure points to its source document and assumptions are printed. The record supports a claim file; it is not legal advice.

**Proof that exists:** The decision record (DR-3105: accountable owner, approve, hold or reject with rationale, history kept, five records attached). Printed assumptions. The "Verified means verified within this snapshot" label. The illustrative Tamberlane notice (NTC-014) and standby rate showing what the record holds.

**Honest limit:** Contract liability between Resonance and a customer is a legal term, not a web claim. The copy answers the part we can show (a wrong input is visible, the owner signs, the source is attached) and does not promise an indemnity. FOUNDER TO CONFIRM the contract position before a sales call, because the CEO will ask for it.

### 7. "How long to set up, and who do you need?"

**Website copy (27 words):**
The first step is a 90-minute working session with the person who knows the late package. No integration project comes first. Later timing is quoted per project.

**Proof that exists:** The working-session format. Internal only: the demo workspace was built in four days, but it ran on synthetic data, so it is not evidence of setup time on a real project and must not be quoted as one.

**FOUNDER TO CONFIRM:** a real number for first connection and hours per week from the customer's side. This is the COO's second question and the page has none.

### 8. "Who else uses it?"

**Website copy (29 words):**
No reference list yet. Check the method instead: the sample hall shows every figure tied to a record ID and every assumption printed. Then test it on your package.

**Proof that exists:** The sample world, labeled fictional on every frame. The method is the proof; the working session is the test.

**Note:** Keep this answer on the deeper page only. On the homepage, the "Sample data. Not a customer." label does the job without raising the question.

**Money question (CFO), held inside 6 and 8:** The sample prices each option (Hold $0, Expedite $1,150,000, Reallocate $410,000; the $740,000 gap is itemised in COM-033) and prints what the figures exclude (financing, revenue, damages, tax). ## 4. How it works, in three steps

1. A package slips. Resonance shows what that does to the handover date and the money. (15)
2. It lists only the recovery options that can actually be built, with costs. (13)
3. One named owner approves an option. The record keeps who approved it, and why. (14)

A COO can repeat these in a corridor: slip, buildable options, one owner signs.
