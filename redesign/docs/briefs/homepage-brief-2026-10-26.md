# Ray's pasted brief, 2026-10-26 (verbatim intent)

Design a Pallet-grade marketing website for Resonance.

WHAT RESONANCE IS: A platform company. One engine — reference ontologies, data agents with evals and guardrails, deterministic constraint solvers and causal inference, and an evidence-linked decision record — runs "decision apps," one per industry. Four verticals: EPC & Data Centers, Supply Chain & Distribution, Infrastructure & Public Works, Housing & Relocation. Customers keep their systems of record (SAP, Oracle, P6, Procore, WMS/TMS, Excel); Resonance sits above them, reads their data as it comes out, and produces decisions a named owner approves.

THE ONE ARGUMENT THE HOMEPAGE MUST WIN IN 30 SECONDS: "The language model is the smallest contributor." (For a sample recovery plan the model cost $1.17; the ontology, solvers and evidence work cost everything else.) The model does two jobs — helps map data to the ontology under evals, and lets people ask the plan questions in plain English. It never decides. The product is: the ontology that knows what a distribution center or a readiness gate is, the solver that refuses an option that can't execute, and the record of who signed and why. The reader to convince is an executive thinking "my people already get this from a chatbot."

THE SIX DISCIPLINES (How it works): 1 Starts with the data you already have (workbook straight out of the ERP). 2 Findings, not silent assumptions. 3 The rules are on screen and in your control (hard vs flexible, ranked). 4 If a rule can't be met, it says which one to relax and what relaxing buys back. 5 One plan, three views (chain, supply, lanes). 6 Exports back into your workbook or ERP, original data untouched.

HERO OPTIONS: "Decisions your operation can actually execute." / "The decision layer on top of the systems you already run." / "Starts with the data you already have. Ends with a decision someone signs."
