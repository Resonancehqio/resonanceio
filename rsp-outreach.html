RSPs are a distinct customer segment from PHAs and developers — they manage projects on behalf of clients and bill per unit. The pitch: Resonance helps RSPs win and retain developer/PHA clients, not just run operations more efficiently.

Messaging Guide
Universal Outreach Principles

What to Say (and What Not to Say)

The RSP market is tiny and word-of-mouth. Every conversation matters. These principles apply across all firms.

Always Lead With
FEC/NYCHA as social proof. Every RSP knows big PACT projects. "We run the FEC relocation program" is the fastest credibility signal in this market. It opens doors that a product description won't.

The specific pain. Don't pitch features. Ask: "What are you using to track notices and URA documentation across your portfolio right now?" Let them name the spreadsheet.
Never Lead With
Pricing. RSPs bill per unit and pass costs through. Price is not the first question — fit is. Get to a demo first.

"Are you using software?" The answer is no, and it makes them feel behind. Ask what they're using instead — they'll tell you the gap.

A feature list. Natalie's reaction wasn't to features. It was to dynamic date scheduling and the Tetris metaphor. Lead with outcomes, not capabilities.
The RSP-Specific Value Prop
RSPs don't just need Resonance to run their operations — they need it to impress their clients. Natalie said this unprompted: "I think this will blow my clients away." That's the real pitch: Resonance is how RSPs differentiate themselves in a word-of-mouth market where the next contract depends on the last project's reputation.
Conference Circuit Strategy
Resonance was discovered at NICHA. The same circuit applies to all five firms: NAHRO Annual Conference, NICHA, and regional affordable housing convenings are where these firms find tools, referrals, and each other.

One RSP referral from ECM can open every other door on this list. Close Natalie first — then ask who else she knows. In a market of 50 firms, one warm intro is worth 10 cold emails.
